Systems Foundation#

I work at the systems level.

That means I don’t start with tools, platforms, or trends — I start with constraints, lifecycles, and interactions between components. Hardware, operating systems, networks, and users are not separate domains to me; they are interdependent parts of a single system that either degrades gracefully or fails catastrophically depending on how it is designed, maintained, and repaired.

Much of my work involves technology that is no longer supported, partially broken, or explicitly written off — not as nostalgia projects, but as practical exercises in recovery, adaptation, and longevity.

Core Principles#

1. Longevity Over Replacement#

I prioritize extending the useful life of systems whenever possible. This includes:

  • Repairing or restoring hardware others consider obsolete
  • Working around driver, firmware, or vendor abandonment
  • Rebuilding systems to meet new purposes rather than discarding them

Longevity forces deeper understanding. When updates stop and documentation disappears, systems thinking becomes the only option.

2. Constraint-Driven Design#

Many of the environments I’ve worked in involved real constraints:

  • Physical layouts that affect network performance
  • Legacy interfaces (FireWire, early SATA, proprietary connectors)
  • Locked or single-purpose devices repurposed for general use
  • Mixed operating systems across eras and architectures

Constraints are not blockers — they are design inputs.

3. Hardware, Software, Network as One System#

I do not treat hardware, operating systems, and networking as silos.

A decision at one layer always impacts the others:

  • A hardware rebuild changes thermal and power behavior
  • OS choices affect driver availability and security posture
  • Network topology determines usability long before bandwidth does

Most failures are not caused by a single broken component, but by mismatches between layers.

Areas of Applied Work#

This foundation is supported by hands-on work across several domains:

  • Hardware teardown, rebuild, and recovery
  • Operating system installation on unsupported or repurposed devices
  • Home and small-scale mesh network design
  • Device reclamation and locked-system repurposing
  • Legacy data recovery and interface bridging

Each of these areas is documented separately, but they all originate from the same systems-first approach.

Why This Matters#

Systems that last are systems that are understood.

Understanding does not come from ideal conditions — it comes from:

  • Diagnosing failures without vendor support
  • Designing around physical and architectural limits
  • Making deliberate tradeoffs instead of default upgrades

This site documents that work not as a timeline of devices, but as a record of systems thinking applied in the real world.
